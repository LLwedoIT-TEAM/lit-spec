# Governance

LIT is developed in the open. The project favors practical interoperability, author agency, and reversible evolution over feature volume.

## Roles

- **Contributors** participate in issues, examples, implementations, and pull requests.
- **Editors** review proposals, keep normative text and schemas aligned, and prepare releases.
- **Maintainers** merge changes, manage releases, and administer the repository.

Roles are earned through sustained, constructive participation. Maintainers may appoint or remove editors and maintainers by a recorded two-thirds vote. A person affected by a role decision does not vote on that decision.

## Decision process

Routine fixes may be merged after one approving maintainer review. Material changes follow this process:

1. Open an issue describing the interoperability problem, use cases, alternatives, and compatibility impact.
2. Allow at least 14 calendar days for public discussion.
3. Submit a pull request updating normative text, schemas, examples, and the changelog.
4. Obtain approval from two maintainers and resolve substantive objections.

The project seeks rough consensus. If consensus cannot be reached, maintainers decide by a two-thirds vote documented on the proposal. Breaking changes require the same threshold and a major version.

## Releases

LIT follows Semantic Versioning. Editors prepare release candidates, confirm schema and example validation, and summarize compatibility impact. A maintainer tags the release after another maintainer approves it.

Security fixes may use an abbreviated private review and immediate patch release. The rationale and impact should be disclosed once doing so is safe.

## Appeals and conflicts of interest

Anyone may ask maintainers to reconsider a decision by presenting new technical or community evidence. Maintainers must disclose material conflicts of interest and recuse themselves when impartiality could reasonably be questioned.

## Conduct

Participation must be respectful, inclusive, and focused on the work. Harassment, threats, doxxing, and sustained disruption are not tolerated. Maintainers may moderate contributions or restrict participation to protect the community, documenting actions when privacy and safety allow.

## Project stewardship

No single vendor controls the format. Governance, schemas, examples, and issue history remain publicly available. If active maintenance ends, maintainers should identify a responsible successor or archive the repository with a clear status notice.
